# Clinical Practice Guidelines for Transfusion Management of Beta-Thalassemia Major
**Issuing Organization:** Indian Academy of Pediatrics (IAP) & National Thalassemia Welfare Society  
**Version:** 2.1  
**Publication Date:** 2021-08-10 | **Effective Date:** 2021-09-01  
**Category:** Thalassemia Care Protocol

## SEC-2.1: Pre-Transfusion Hemoglobin Targets and Regimen (Page 14)
The universally recommended hypertransfusion protocol for pediatric and adult Thalassemia Major patients aims to maintain pre-transfusion hemoglobin between 9.5 and 10.5 g/dL. This target suppresses ineffective erythropoiesis, prevents skeletal deformities, and inhibits excessive intestinal iron absorption. Transfusions should be scheduled every 2 to 4 weeks depending on the rate of hemoglobin decline. Post-transfusion hemoglobin should ideally not exceed 14.0 g/dL to prevent hyperviscosity.

*Keywords: pre-transfusion hemoglobin, target hemoglobin, hypertransfusion, thalassemia major, transfusion schedule, erythropoiesis*

## SEC-3.4: Extended Phenotyping and Alloimmunization Prevention (Page 22)
Red cell alloimmunization develops in 15% to 37% of chronically transfused Thalassemia patients. Prior to the very first transfusion, extended red cell antigen phenotyping must be performed for ABO, Rh (D, C, c, E, e) and Kell (K, k) systems. At minimum, transfused blood must be matched for ABO, RhD, C, c, E, e, and Kell. Matching for Rh and Kell antigens reduces alloimmunization rates to below 5%. If a patient develops Anti-Kell, all future units must be verified Kell-negative. If a patient develops Anti-E or Anti-C, corresponding antigen-negative blood is mandatory.

*Keywords: alloimmunization, extended phenotyping, Kell antigen, Rh antigens, anti-E, anti-K, minor antigen matching*

## SEC-4.1: Blood Component Specifications: Leukoreduction and Shelf-Life (Page 31)
Thalassemia patients must receive leukoreduced packed red blood cells (residual leukocytes < 1 x 10^6 per unit). Pre-storage leukofiltration within 48 hours of collection is preferred over bedside filtration. Fresh red blood cells stored for fewer than 7 to 14 days in additive solution (SAGM) are recommended to ensure optimal 2,3-DPG levels and maximum in vivo red cell survival, thereby reducing transfusion frequency and secondary iron accumulation.

*Keywords: leukoreduction, leukodepletion, fresh blood, red cell age, SAGM, 2,3-DPG, filtration*

## SEC-5.2: Iron Overload Monitoring and Chelation Initiation (Page 45)
Each milliliter of transfused packed red blood cells delivers approximately 1.0 to 1.16 mg of elemental iron. Serum ferritin should be monitored every 3 months. Iron chelation therapy should be initiated when serum ferritin persistently exceeds 1,000 ng/mL, or after the patient has received 10 to 20 blood transfusions (typically around 2 to 3 years of age). First-line oral iron chelators include Deferasirox (20-40 mg/kg/day once daily) or Deferiprone (75-100 mg/kg/day in three divided doses). Annual liver iron concentration (LIC by Ferriscan/MRI T2*) and cardiac T2* MRI are recommended for patients aged >= 7 years.

*Keywords: iron overload, serum ferritin, chelation therapy, deferasirox, deferiprone, cardiac T2*, ferritin monitoring*
