## SEC-14.3: Identification and Management of Multiple Alloantibodies (Page 312)
When a patient has documented red cell alloantibodies (e.g., anti-E, anti-K, anti-Fya, anti-Jka), units selected for transfusion must be antigen-negative by serological typing using licensed reagent antisera. An indirect antiglobulin test (IAT) crossmatch through the 37°C incubation and anti-human globulin (AHG) phase is mandatory; electronic crossmatch is strictly prohibited. If multiple antibodies make compatible units scarce, rare donor registries and regional reference laboratories must be mobilized.

*Keywords: alloantibodies, IAT crossmatch, AHG phase, antigen negative units, electronic crossmatch contraindication, rare donor registry*

## SEC-15.1: Indications for Irradiated and Washed Cellular Blood Components (Page 348)
Gamma or X-ray irradiation (minimum 25 Gy to the central plane) is mandatory for blood components transfused to allogeneic or autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients, patients receiving directed donations from blood relatives, and patients receiving HLA-matched components to prevent Transfusion-Associated Graft-versus-Host Disease (TA-GvHD). Washing red blood cells with 0.9% sterile saline is indicated for patients with documented severe or recurrent anaphylactic transfusion reactions, IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies, or neonatal hyperkalemia risk.

*Keywords: irradiated blood, TA-GvHD, stem cell transplant, directed donation, washed red cells, anaphylactic reaction, IgA deficiency*

## SEC-16.2: Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reactions (DHTR) & Hyperhemolysis Protocol (Page 384)
Delayed hemolytic transfusion reactions occur 3 to 14 days post-transfusion due to anamnestic secondary immune response against minor antigens (commonly Kidd [Jka, Jkb], Rh, or Duffy). Symptoms include unexplained hemoglobin drop, jaundice, fever, and spherocytosis on blood smear. In rare cases, hyperhemolysis syndrome develops, where both donor and autologous patient red cells are destroyed. Transfusion in hyperhemolysis must be avoided if possible; high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) and systemic corticosteroids (methylprednisolone) are the primary therapeutic modalities.

*Keywords: delayed hemolytic reaction, DHTR, hyperhemolysis, kidd system, IVIG, anamnestic response*
